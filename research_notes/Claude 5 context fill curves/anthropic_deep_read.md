### Inferences
- The compaction-enabled ProgramBench curves show score still rising after the 1M-token mark: Opus 5.5 from roughly 0.87 at 1M cumulative tokens to roughly 0.97 at about 20M. Anthropic does not describe this as evidence about post-compaction quality; that reading is mine. The curves are cumulative "best state of the repository if stopped here" averages over tasks, and the population thins as tasks finish, so they are progress curves, not per-token quality curves. They do not show a collapse after the first window's worth of tokens.
- Comparing Table 1 with Table 2 suggests the single-window ceiling (Opus 5.5 91.2%, Fable 5.2 85.6%, Sonnet 5.5 79.7%) is below what the same models reach with compaction and more tokens (about 0.97, 0.94 within 4 hours, 0.917 within 4 hours). The cards explicitly say the two setups are not directly comparable, so this is suggestive only.
- The secret-keeping curves measure survival under adversarial pressure, so decline with turns reflects accumulating attack attempts rather than passive forgetting of an instruction. They are the only Anthropic figures for these three models with a turn-count x-axis.
- "Character drift" is a single averaged audit score per model, not a curve; all three models sit near the floor of the scale (1.04–1.07).

### Gaps
- No score by context-fill bin (for example below 200K, 200K–500K, 500K–1M) for any of the three models, on any benchmark. Confirmed by reading, not only by keyword search.
- The underlying numbers for the score-versus-tokens and score-versus-latency curves are not published as tables; values above were read by eye.
- The compaction trigger threshold, the number of compactions per run, and the summarisation prompt used in the 20M-token single-agent ProgramBench runs are not stated.
- Sonnet 5.5's ProgramBench context-exhaustion rate and its shown-remaining-tokens score are not published. The F52 card's comparison covers only Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1.
- ExploitGym 2 h / 6 h counts for Sonnet 5.5 are in a figure only; the per-length (one- and three-week) influence-campaign scores are not printed.

## 2. What exactly does each card's long-context section measure, with what harness, and what does it say about failure near the limit?

### Takeaway
In all three cards the section titled "Long context" contains one benchmark, ProgramBench, reported as a single aggregate from a single 1M-token window with no compaction, on the upstream mini-swe-agent harness with the time limit removed. Only the Fable 5.2 card says anything about the limit itself: the harness gave "no indication of how much remains", Fable 5.2 ran out of context before submitting on 68% of tasks, and showing remaining tokens lifted it from 85.6% to 91.6% while Opus 5.5 was unchanged.

### Cited Findings
- Benchmark definition (near-identical wording in all three cards): "ProgramBench is a long-context agentic coding benchmark of 200 program reconstruction tasks. Given only a binary compiled from an open-source project and that project's documentation, the agent must rebuild a codebase that reproduces the original program's behavior without using the internet or decompilation tools. Tasks range from small terminal utilities (jq, ripgrep) to large systems (FFmpeg, SQLite, the PHP interpreter). Submissions are graded against more than 247,000 execution-based behavioral tests generated via agent-driven fuzzing." (F52 card p.215; O55 card p.183; S55 card p.117) — [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Task filter and harness: "We excluded 34 tasks for which the reference binary itself scored below 0.9 on the hidden test suite (indicating test flakiness), leaving 166 tasks. On those tasks, we scored only against tests the reference binary passes. We report the hidden-test pass rate using the same mini-swe-agent harness that the upstream repo uses, without the six-hour time limit." (F52 card pp.215–216) — [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Near-limit behaviour, the only such passage in the three cards: "The harness gives the model a single 1M-token context window with no indication of how much remains. Fable 5.2 ran out of context before submitting on 68% of tasks (compared with 39% for Opus 5.5 and 28% for Fable 5.1). When the harness also showed the number of context tokens remaining after each step, Fable 5.2 scored 91.6% (up from 85.6%), compared with Fable 5.1 at 86.8% and Opus 5.5 at 91.1%." (F52 card p.216) — [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Anthropic's own claim about coverage of lengths, with no distribution given: "We believe ProgramBench is a strong measure of long-context coding performance: Fable 5.2's episodes cover a range of context lengths up to the full 1M-token window, and the tasks test long-context capabilities that closely align with real-world use cases." (F52 card p.216). The Opus 5.5 (p.184) and Sonnet 5.5 (p.118) cards carry the same sentence with the model name swapped and "practical downstream use cases" — [O55 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5-system-card); [S55 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5-system-card)
- Opus 5.5 card result sentence: "On this set, Claude Opus 5.5 scored 91.2%, whereas Claude Fable 5.1 scored 87.6% and Claude Opus 5 scored 85.4%." (p.183). ### Inferences
- With 68% of Fable 5.2's blind runs hitting the wall and a 6.0-point recovery when the remaining count is shown, the single-window ProgramBench deficit for Fable 5.2 looks like a pacing problem (working until the window is gone without submitting) more than a loss of capability at high fill. The card reports the numbers but does not state a cause.
- The blind ProgramBench condition matches the default API situation for all three models, since none receives context-awareness tags; the shown-remaining condition corresponds to a harness that prints the count or to task budgets.
- The ProgramBench finding and the prompting-guide finding are not contradictory: one is about a remaining-context counter on a task that fills the window, the other about a large token-budget line in the prompt on ordinary coding tasks. Anthropic does not reconcile them in either document.

### Gaps
- No distribution of final context lengths across ProgramBench episodes, and no score split between episodes that hit the limit and those that did not.
- Effort level for the single-window ProgramBench runs is not stated in any of the three cards.
- What happens on a task when the window runs out before submission (scored as zero, or graded on the repository state at that point) is not stated.
- The two long-context benchmark families behind the AECI fit are unnamed and unscored in public.

## 3. What does Anthropic say, with numbers, about these models' long-running autonomous work (hours on task, sessions, tokens)?

### Takeaway
There is a good deal of hours-scale evidence, most of it about multi-agent or compaction-enabled runs rather than one uninterrupted context: single Opus 5.5 agents scored over 24-hour runs, a 1,000-agent Fable 5.2 team proving Fermat's Last Theorem in 28.7 hours, benchmark budgets of 3 to 20 hours, and launch-post anecdotes of 9.5- to 18-hour unattended runs. Alongside it Anthropic publishes concrete early-stopping numbers for Mythos 5.2 / Fable 5.2 and says its alignment testing has thin coverage of very long trajectories and of compaction.

**Docs: what is stated about quality versus context size, and the thresholds**
- "more context isn't automatically better. As token count grows, accuracy and recall degrade, a phenomenon known as *context rot*." No model named, no numbers. — [Context windows](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows)
- Compaction "keeps the active context small, because response quality degrades as a conversation grows." Threshold compaction "starts at 150,000 input tokens by default"; the `trigger` "must be at least 50,000 tokens". Supported models include `claude-fable-5-2`, `claude-opus-5-5` and `claude-sonnet-5-5`. With custom `instructions`, on Fable 5.2, Mythos 5.2, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 the request "summarizes from the visible conversation only, without earlier thinking blocks". — [Compaction overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/compaction); [Compaction at a token threshold](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/compaction-threshold)
- Context editing: "context is a finite resource with diminishing returns, and irrelevant content degrades model focus." Tool-result clearing default trigger: 100,000 input tokens. — [Context editing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-editing)
- Preserved thinking makes history editing costly on these models: for Fable 5.2, "In Anthropic's testing, when earlier thinking was dropped on every turn, the model thought again and used more output tokens." Client-side pruning of old tool results is advised against on Fable 5.2, Mythos 5.2, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5; "Use compaction instead". — [Prompting Claude Fable 5.2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-2); [Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Cost measurements of context levers were made "on an earlier model", not on these three: "context editing adds 73% on the short run; compaction saves 32% and pruning 39% on the long". — [Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Defect-search benchmark for "work larger than one context window": "a corpus of about 22 million tokens from 14 public Python package sources, in 201 parts, with 130 planted defects". "Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagents cost about a third less than one Claude Opus 5.5 session per part at its default effort, and scored about the same. Alone, from `low` to `high`, Claude Fable 5.2 scored 1.6 to 5.9 points higher than Claude Opus 5.5 alone at `high`, for 1.5 to 2.4 times the cost". — [Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Effort guidance tied to run length: `xhigh` is for "Long-running agentic and coding tasks (over 30 minutes) with token budgets in the millions". Fable 5.2 is described as "For demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work", Opus 5.5 as "For long-running agentic coding and knowledge work". — [Effort](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort)
- Claude Code: "Most current models have a 1M-token window and compaction happens on its own, so running out is rare. `/clear` between tasks is still worth doing, because a focused context gets you better answers and costs less." "Running out of context is rarely the problem anymore." After compaction: "The summary keeps your requests and key code snippets, but detailed instructions from early in the conversation may be lost. Put persistent rules in CLAUDE.md." Instruction files: "target under 200 lines per CLAUDE.md file. Longer files consume more context and reduce adherence." — [Best practices for Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices); [How Claude Code works](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/how-claude-code-works); [How Claude remembers your project](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Starting fresh versus compacting: "When a context window is cleared, consider starting with a brand new context window rather than using compaction. Claude's latest models are extremely effective at discovering state from the local filesystem." No model named, no numbers. — [Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)

### Inferences
- Almost all of Anthropic's hours-and-days claims for these models describe work spread over many context windows (subagents, teams, compaction, sessions directing sessions). The 24-hour single-agent Opus 5.5 scores and the compaction-enabled ProgramBench curves are the only quantitative single-agent long-run results, and both necessarily involve compaction or equivalent context turnover, although only ProgramBench says so.
- Anthropic's published failure numbers for long autonomous work on Fable 5.2 are about stopping too early (63% of research sessions quit without an experiment at medium effort; 6–13% of coding sessions left work undone), not about quality decay late in the window.
- The alignment post gives the only Anthropic numbers on how one of these models (Opus 5.5) behaves when dropped into a multi-compaction transcript: it follows inherited compaction notes much less than Mythos 5 did (0–13% versus 83%) but rarely breaks a trajectory once inside it (10% spontaneous stops). The setting is off-policy and about harmful momentum, not task accuracy.
- Anthropic's docs assert quality degradation with context growth in general terms on three separate pages, while the Claude Code best-practices page tells users that running out of context "is rarely the problem anymore". Neither position is backed by a published measurement on Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 or Fable 5.2.

### Gaps
- No hours-on-task or session-length measurement for a single uncompacted context for any of the three models.
- No before-versus-after-compaction score, and no measurement of instruction retention or recall after compaction, for any of the three models.
- The agent-memory post's CL-Bench figure values for Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 are in a chart that was not rendered; the text gives no numbers.
- The "long-horizon coding benchmark" behind the "78% less per solved task" claim is linked but was not opened.
- Customer names attached to launch-post quotes were not captured in the extracted text, so quotes above are left unattributed.
- Posts were found by reading the anthropic.com/engineering, /research and /news index pages, claude.dev/blog, claude.com/resources/articles and alignment.anthropic.com. The Anthropic cookbook repository and any posts not linked from those indexes were not checked.

## 4. Per model: what is and is not published

### Takeaway
For all three models Anthropic publishes a 1M-token window, one single-window ProgramBench aggregate, a compaction-enabled score-versus-tokens chart, turn-axis secret-keeping curves and a character-drift score. For none of them does it publish accuracy by context-fill level, a recall or retrieval test at length, or a before-and-after compaction comparison. Fable 5.2 has the most near-limit evidence, Sonnet 5.5 the least.

### Cited Findings

**Claude Fable 5.2 / Claude Mythos 5.2 (one underlying model)**
- Published: ProgramBench 85.6% blind and 91.6% with remaining tokens shown; 68% of blind tasks ran out of the 1M window (F52 card pp.215–216). Compaction-enabled single-agent curve to about 0.94 by 1.5–2M tokens within a four-hour budget (Fig. 8.12.1.B p.223, chart-read). Secret-keeping over turns: 66% and 23% at the end of 20-turn pressure (p.172), password survival curve to 140 turns (p.169). Character drift 1.043 (p.120). ARC-AGI-3 79.26% versus 97.72% with provider context management (p.243). Early stopping: 63% and 6–13% (p.34). Hours-scale team results at 18 and 30 hours (pp.225–227). Docs: early-stopping prompt halves unfinished endings at +30% output tokens; removing a large in-prompt token budget "lowered cost, with equivalent performance". — [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card); [Prompting Claude Fable 5.2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-2)
- The long-context numbers are attributed to "Claude Fable 5.2"; the early-stopping and secret-keeping numbers to "Claude Mythos 5.2". The card describes them as "two configurations of a new large language model" (p.2). — [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Not published: any score by context length; the two "long-context benchmark families" named or scored (p.36); the distribution of episode lengths; exhaustion rate in the shown-remaining condition; any post-compaction quality measure.

**Claude Opus 5.5**
- Published: ProgramBench 91.2% blind (O55 card p.183); 39% exhaustion and 91.1% with remaining tokens shown, both only in the later Fable card (F52 p.216). Compaction-enabled single-agent curve from about 0.87 at 1M to about 0.97 near 20M cumulative tokens (O55 Fig. 8.12.1.B p.191, chart-read). Single-agent 24-hour runs: 0.37 / 0.47 / 0.53 and 0.07 / 0.14 / 0.39 at 2 / 6 / 24 h (pp.196–197). ExploitGym 289 at 2 h, 300 at 6 h (p.54). OSWorld with compaction above 100k tokens: 81.8% / 48.7% (p.206). Compaction-message refusals in under 0.01% of completions (p.102). Character drift 1.04–1.07 across three cards. Secret-keeping curves in the Fable card (chart-read; about 0.73 hidden-reasoning and 0.17 shown-reasoning at 20 turns). Alignment post: 0–13% attack initiation from compaction notes, 10% spontaneous stops in later compaction cycles. Launch post: 9.5-hour HAProxy rewrite, "over 18 hours" customer run. — [O55 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5-system-card); [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card); [Alignment assessment post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-assessment-ukaisi); [Opus 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)
- Not published: any score by context length; any statement in its own card about behaviour at the limit; time budget and compaction settings behind the 20M-token curve; any post-compaction quality measure.

**Claude Sonnet 5.5**
- Published: ProgramBench 79.7% blind (S55 card pp.117–118). Compaction-enabled single agent at 1 / 2 / 4 hours, about 0.856 / 0.896 / 0.917 at roughly 0.4M / 0.7M / 1.35M average tokens (Figs. 8.12.1.A–B pp.123–124, chart-read). OSWorld with compaction above 100k tokens: 80.1% / 43.5% (p.130). Copying inserted faults from its own earlier turns in 62% of sessions (p.98). Character drift 1.05–1.07. Secret-keeping curves in the Fable card (chart-read; weakest of the three: about 0.58 hidden-reasoning and 0.03 shown-reasoning at 20 turns; password gone in nearly all investigations by turn 26). FrontierSWE v2 61.9% on 20-hour tasks (p.114). — [S55 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5-system-card); [F52 card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Not published: ProgramBench context-exhaustion rate; score with remaining tokens shown; any score by context length; any near-limit statement; any post-compaction quality measure. The S55 card has no pilot-use or internal-deployment-monitoring section, so the session-level observations available for the other two models do not exist for it.

**All three**
- 1M-token context window by default and 128k max output per Messages API request; compaction, context editing and task budgets supported; none receives context-awareness tags. — [Context windows](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows); [Task budgets](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/task-budgets)

### Inferences
- A context-fill accuracy curve for these three models still cannot be drawn from Anthropic sources. What can now be drawn from primary material, with chart-read caveats, is: a score-versus-cumulative-tokens progress curve under compaction for each model; a blind-versus-informed pair with exhaustion rates for Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5; turn-axis secret-keeping curves for all three; and 2 / 6 / 24-hour single-agent points for Opus 5.5.
- On the turn-axis and long-run measures available, the ordering is consistent: Opus 5.5 and Mythos 5.2 hold up better than Sonnet 5.5, and Fable 5.2's distinctive weakness is pacing against an unseen limit and early stopping, which Anthropic reports as responsive to harness and prompt changes.

- The Claude Haiku 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 cards were not re-read for this pass (out of scope); the Haiku 5.5 card was only checked for cross-references and added nothing on the three models' context behaviour.
- Whether Anthropic's Transparency Hub or model pages carry additional length-binned results was not checked.
