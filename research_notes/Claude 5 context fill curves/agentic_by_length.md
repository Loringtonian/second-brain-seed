# Agentic-benchmark evidence on length / context effects: Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.2 (= Mythos 5.2)

Research pass of 25 Nov 2026. Scope: only Opus 5.5 (released 22 Sep 2026), Sonnet 5.5 (28 Sep 2026), Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2 (9 Nov 2026). Every number below comes from a page or file I opened. Rows marked **DERIVED** are my own arithmetic on a publisher's raw data (method stated); they are not figures the publisher printed. Nothing is read off a chart by eye.

**Headline:** no source publishes "same task, same model, context at 10% / 50% / 90% full". The closest measured evidence is (a) Anthropic's own ProgramBench context-exhaustion rates in a single 1M window, (b) Epoch's one-run Vending-Bench 2 swap from a 69K window to 900K compaction, (c) ARC Prize's two harnesses that differ in context handling, (d) Proximal's FrontierSWE v2 per-run traces (context refills to ~850K, then compaction), (e) METR's success-vs-human-task-length data for Opus 5.5 only.

## For each of the three models: is there a measured relationship between task/trajectory length (or tokens in context) and success?

### Takeaway
Only Opus 5.5 has a published success-vs-task-length curve (METR): near-perfect on tasks that take humans minutes, about 80% around 4 human-hours, roughly a coin flip at 30 to 50 human-hours, with a very wide interval. For Sonnet 5.5 and Fable 5.2 the only length-related evidence is FrontierSWE v2's per-run traces, where all three models repeatedly fill the context to about 850K tokens and compact, and score does not fall with more compactions, steps or tokens once task difficulty is controlled. Task length here means human time or run length, not a controlled measure of context fill.

### Inferences
- For Opus 5.5 the METR curve is the only quantified "longer task, lower success" relationship. It is success versus how long the task takes a human, so it mixes difficulty, number of steps and context load; it cannot be read as "performance at X% context fill".
- The METR bucket table shows a cliff between tasks of 4-16 human-hours (about 83%) and 16-48 human-hours (about 35%), but the long buckets rest on 10 and 5 tasks, mostly from the new TH2 families, so the step may be about which tasks are long as much as about length.
- In FrontierSWE, runs that went through more compactions (each one a refill to about 850K tokens) did not score lower than same-task runs with fewer. This is observational: models that are making progress keep working, so a positive association is expected even if high fill hurts a little.
- METR's token curves are "best score so far", which can only go up; they show whether extra tokens still buy progress, not whether quality degrades late in a run.

### Gaps
- No METR time horizon or task-length breakdown exists for Sonnet 5.5 or Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2. METR's public time-horizons page stopped updating on 8 Sep 2026 and its data file ends at "Claude Mythos Preview (early)" (https://metr.org/time-horizons/). The Fable 5.2 card says METR tested Mythos 5.2, but I found no METR numbers for it.
- METR does not publish runs per task for these panels, tokens per run, or how often its scaffold compacted.
- No source gives success binned by tokens actually in context at the time of an action, for any of the three models.
- Terminal-Bench, SWE-bench Pro, Vals, Artificial Analysis and CursorBench publish no per-task trajectory-length or duration breakdown for these models. The Scale SWE-bench Pro leaderboard page was not opened; search results showed no entries or failure analysis for these models.
- OpenHands Index, Aider, LiveSWEBench, Cline/Roo/Kilo, Cognition: searches returned nothing for these three models; pages not opened, so absence is UNVERIFIED.

## Any measured comparison of context-window or compaction settings for these models?

### Takeaway
Three exist, none clean. Anthropic's ProgramBench result is the strongest: in one 1M window with no compaction, Fable 5.2 ran out of context on 68% of tasks and gained 6 points when simply shown its remaining context, while Opus 5.5 ran out on 39% and was unaffected. Epoch's Vending-Bench 2 test (69K window with deletion vs compaction at 900K) is a single run per condition, and Epoch itself attributes Opus 5.5's 44% drop to strategy, not context. ARC-AGI-3's two harnesses show very large gains for Sonnet 5.5 and Fable 5.2 when reasoning is preserved and history compacted instead of trimmed.

### Cited Findings
- ProgramBench (Anthropic's version: 166 of 200 tasks, mini-swe-agent, no six-hour limit, hidden-test pass rate): "The harness gives the model a single 1M-token context window with no indication of how much remains. Fable 5.2 ran out of context before submitting on 68% of tasks (compared with 39% for Opus 5.5 and 28% for Fable 5.1). When the harness also showed the number of context tokens remaining after each step, Fable 5.2 scored 91.6% (up from 85.6%), compared with Fable 5.1 at 86.8% and Opus 5.5 at 91.1%." — [Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2 system card, sec. 8.10.1](https://www.anthropic.com/document/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Same benchmark, no display: Opus 5.5 91.2%, Sonnet 5.5 79.7%, Fable 5.2 85.6%. Anthropic says episodes "cover a range of context lengths up to the full 1M token window". — [Opus 5.5 system card sec. 8.10.1](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf), [Sonnet 5.5 system card sec. 8.10.1](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5-system-card)
- Anthropic's multi-agent ProgramBench section reruns the single agent with "context compaction and a 20M token budget" instead of the 1M budget, but states those scores "are not directly comparable" with section 8.10.1 because tools and grading differ, and it prints curves, not a final number. — [Opus 5.5 system card sec. 8.12.1](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf)
- Vals AI's ProgramBench uses a stricter "fully resolved" metric: Fable 5.2 19.00% ± 2.78, Opus 5.5 18.50% ± 2.75, Sonnet 5.5 6.50% ± 1.75. - Epoch's Vending-Bench 2 review (dated 21 Oct 2026, verdict "Flawed"): the official harness tells the agent it has a 69,000-token window, counts tokens with OpenAI's tiktoken, and when full deletes the oldest 39% of messages "without a summary"; this happened 114 times in the Opus run and 86 times for GPT-6 Astra, whose effective limit was about 45,000 tokens. — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review)
- Epoch's elicitation gave both models "larger context windows and auto-compaction": "Context was compacted automatically at 250,000 tokens for Astra and 900,000 for Opus. We ran Opus on the Anthropic API instead of Amazon Bedrock." Results: Opus 5.5 official run $9,459 (leaderboard average $9,235 ± $785), elicitation $5,254; "Opus earned 44% less than its official run, Astra earned 17% more" ($15,366 to $18,044). — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review)
- Epoch's explanation for Opus: "This result seems entirely based on a worse strategy. Context had no discernible impact." — [Epoch review, footnote 6](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review)
- Same review: in the official Opus runs on Bedrock the one-tool-at-a-time setting "had no effect"; Opus sent multiple calls in 126 turns and the harness discarded 238 tool calls. — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review)
- Andon Labs' page: context "limited to roughly 69000 tokens", about 61% of messages kept on trim, 3,000 to 6,000 messages and 60 to 100 million output tokens per run; Sonnet 5.5 averages $11,165.28 ± $1,585 (rank 10 of 77). Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 are in the page's chart legend but their rows sit behind "Show 67 more", so I could not read their numbers there. — [Andon Labs Vending-Bench 2](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)
- ARC-AGI-3 harnesses: Standard "enables a model to carry forward notes it chooses to keep"; Provider Adapter "preserves opaque reasoning state between requests and uses compaction for longer conversations". — [ARC Prize, Fable 5.2 results](https://arcprize.org/results/anthropic-claude-fable-5-2), [ARC Prize blog, 3 Sep 2026](https://arcprize.org/blog/astra)
- Epoch's ARC-AGI-3 review (30 Oct 2026) adds that in the Standard harness "Reasoning is discarded after every action" and "history is trimmed at 175,000 characters, roughly 10-14 frames of memory", and that "every model evaluated at the same effort level under both harnesses scored substantially higher with the 'Provider Adapter' harness." — [Epoch ARC-AGI-3 review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/arc-agi-3/review)

At max effort with the Standard harness, 28 of 55 games ran to the 12-hour limit. — [ARC Prize, Fable 5.2](https://arcprize.org/results/anthropic-claude-fable-5-2); the Anthropic card reports 79.26% / 97.72% at high and 65.66% / 94.90% at max — [Fable 5.2 system card sec. 8.15](https://www.anthropic.com/document/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)

Opus 5.5 has no ARC-AGI-3 score. — [ARC Prize, Sonnet 5.5](https://arcprize.org/results/anthropic-claude-sonnet-5-5), [ARC Prize, Opus 5.5](https://arcprize.org/results/anthropic-claude-opus-5-5)

- Anthropic says Terminal-Bench 4.0 "reduced the confounding role of various harnesses (e.g., CLI memory footprint, compaction strategy, and container communication protocol)". — [Opus 5.5 system card sec. 8.5](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf)

### Inferences
- The ProgramBench pair is the one controlled result showing a context-management effect for a target model: Fable 5.2's 6-point loss came from not knowing how much window was left, not from reasoning worse in a full window. With the display it matched Opus 5.5 (91.6 vs 91.1). Opus 5.5 scored the same either way despite running out on 39% of tasks.
- The Vending-Bench comparison changes several things at once (window size, deletion vs summarisation, API provider, live web suppliers) and has one run per arm against a run-to-run standard error of $785 on the leaderboard. It does not support "Opus 5.5 gets worse with a bigger context"; Epoch says as much.
- The ARC-AGI-3 gap is about losing reasoning state and old frames versus keeping them through compaction. It shows these models do far better with provider-style context management than with aggressive trimming; it says nothing about degradation as a 1M window fills.
- No one has published a 200K vs 1M, or early vs late auto-compact, comparison for these models in Claude Code or any coding harness.

### Gaps
- No published same-harness sweep of compaction threshold or window size for Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 or Fable 5.2. The closest paper, "Benchmarks Are Surprisingly Compaction-Friendly" (arXiv 2610.31778v2, 20 Nov 2026), sweeps thresholds from 160K to 380K in OpenCode but tests Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.3-Codex, GPT-5.4 and Gemini 3.1 Pro, so its numbers are out of scope (https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31778v2).
- Sonnet 5.5's ProgramBench context-exhaustion rate and its score with the remaining-context display are not published.
- Epoch did not publish Vending-Bench elicitation runs for Sonnet 5.5 or Fable 5.2, or more than one elicitation run for Opus 5.5.
- Claude Code auto-compact thresholds for these models: only user bug reports and unofficial write-ups surfaced in search, none with measurements on these models; not opened, UNVERIFIED.

## Any reported failure-mode breakdown mentioning context overflow, forgetting instructions, or post-compaction errors for these models, with rates?

### Takeaway
Rates exist for context exhaustion (ProgramBench: 68% Fable 5.2, 39% Opus 5.5) and for message deletion events in Vending-Bench 2 (114 for Opus 5.5), plus one small study where Opus 5.5 wrote the compaction summary and 45% of downstream repairs broke. No evaluator has published a rate of forgotten instructions or post-compaction errors in long transcripts for any of the three models, and Anthropic lists very long trajectories and compaction as a thin spot in its own alignment testing.

### Cited Findings
- Context exhaustion: Fable 5.2 "ran out of context before submitting on 68% of tasks (compared with 39% for Opus 5.5 ...)" on ProgramBench in a single 1M window. — [Fable 5.2 system card sec. 8.10.1](https://www.anthropic.com/document/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Forced forgetting in Vending-Bench 2: the harness deleted the oldest 39% of messages 114 times in the Opus 5.5 run; Epoch still found "Context had no discernible impact" on the Opus elicitation result. — [Epoch review](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/vending-bench-2/review)
- Compact-Bench (USTC, arXiv 2610.31710v1, 22 Oct 2026): with Claude Opus 5.5 as the summariser using the Claude Code compaction prompt and Kimi-K3 as the continuing agent, 10 of 22 validated SWE-bench repairs (45%, 95% Wilson CI 27 to 65) that passed with full history failed when continued from the summary; stable across three runs. Opus 4.8 damaged 1 of 22 (5%); the range across 14 summarisers was 5% to 64%. Sessions were short (history cached after at most 10 steps, eight-step continuation), and the continuing agent was not a Claude model. — [Compact-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31710v1)
- Anthropic's stated blind spot, repeated in all three cards: "Very long trajectories: Models may take many turns to be talked into misaligned actions ... Context compaction can further change how models act. We simulate compaction, but the space of possible ways of implementing any given scenario in a long transcript is far too large for us to explore thoroughly." The Opus 5.5 card adds that the automated behavioural audit "currently has relatively thin coverage of multi-agent scenarios and tasks with very long trajectories." The Sonnet 5.5 card: "we have more limited coverage of genuinely long trajectories." — [Opus 5.5 system card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf), [Sonnet 5.5 system card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5-system-card), [Fable 5.2 system card](https://www.anthropic.com/document/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-system-card)
- Opus 5.5 card, internal deployment monitoring: "Rare instances in which other snapshots of Claude Opus 5.5 running as a specialized subagent would refuse to write a compaction message. We saw this in less than 0.01% of completions, according to our automated offline pipeline." One instance replied "I'll skip the summary, since it would mean writing out my earlier reasoning," then continued its task. — [Opus 5.5 system card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf)
- METR's failure notes for the Q4 report concern reward hacking (about 10 official runs, under 0.1%, set to zero; Opus 5.5 among the models flagged), refusals (about 36 runs excluded) and under-use of token budgets. It reports no context-overflow or forgetting category. — [METR Q4 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)
- ClickHouse's failure split for Sonnet 5.5 (63 failures of 201): 75% wrong plan, 19% wrong data selection, 6% wrong implementation; zero runs truncated by the 60-turn budget for either Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5. Runs are short (mean 3.5 turns), so this says nothing about long contexts. — [ClickHouse benchmark, 20 Oct 2026](https://clickhouse.com/blog/agentic-analytics-benchmark-round-two)
- Vals AI reports fallback and refusal rates, not context failures: Opus 5.5 fallback 2.68%, refusal 0.87%; Sonnet 5.5 fallback 3.99%, refusal 0.22%; Fable 5.2 fallback 1.88%, refusal 0.00%. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, counting fallback-assisted tasks as failures drops Opus 5.5 from 61.62% to 53.54% (30 of 198) and Sonnet 5.5 from 53.03% to 50.51% (7 of 198). — [Vals Opus 5.5](https://vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-opus-5-5), [Vals Sonnet 5.5](https://vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-sonnet-5-5), [Vals Fable 5.2](https://vals.ai/models/anthropic_claude-fable-5-2)
- Sonnet 5.5 launch post footnote: at max effort on FrontierCode it "more often ran Claude Code's code-review skill, which split reviews across subagents", causing timeouts or out-of-scope edits in two cases. A harness-interaction failure, not context overflow. — [Anthropic, Sonnet 5.5 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5-5)

### Inferences
- "Ran out of context" on ProgramBench is a planning failure (not budgeting the window) more than a quality-decay failure; the remaining-context display fixed it for Fable 5.2.
- The Compact-Bench result is about the quality of an Opus 5.5-written summary handed to a different, weaker agent after a handful of steps. It is weak evidence about what happens when Opus 5.5 continues from its own compaction in a long run; FrontierSWE's compaction counts (Table 3) point the other way for real long runs.
- Because safeguard fallbacks hand some tasks to older models (Opus 5, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5), part of each published agentic score for Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 was produced by a different model. Any length analysis built on those leaderboards inherits that noise.

### Gaps
- No rate of instruction loss, constraint erosion or post-compaction mistakes has been published for Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 or Fable 5.2 in their own long runs.
- Apollo, Transluce, Palisade: searches returned no reports on long transcripts with these models. UK AISI's work in the Fable 5.2 card concerns monitorability and sandbox escape, not transcript length.
- User reports of long-session drift that surfaced in search concern Opus 4.6 and Opus 5, not the target models; not opened.

### Inferences
- The evidence base is thin and uneven: Opus 5.5 has three independent length-related sources (METR, Epoch, Proximal), Fable 5.2 has two plus Anthropic's ablation, Sonnet 5.5 has only ARC Prize and Proximal.

### Gaps
- Not opened (search only, nothing relevant surfaced): Scale SEAL leaderboards, OpenHands Index live site, Aider, LiveSWEBench, Cline/Roo/Kilo evals, Cursor's CursorBench 4.0 post, Cognition, Apollo, Transluce, Palisade, Zapier AutomationBench reports, Snorkel, Sonar, Endor Labs.
